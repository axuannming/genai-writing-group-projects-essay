# When Shortcuts Harm the Team:  Generative AI Writing Tools Do More Damage Than Good in Hong Kong College Group Projects

When university students can generate an entire group project report in seconds using AI, is genuine teamwork still possible?  With the rapid development of technology, AI writing tools such as ChatGPT have become very popular between universities because they can generate reports and assignments in just a few seconds. At first, some local universities banned them, while others allowed students to use them under strict rules, and the policies are still no consensus reached today (Mok, 2023). UNESCO (2023) has also convey that the rise of generative AI changes the method of learning, teachers' grading, and how universities need to protect academic honesty. Although generative AI writing tools may appear to improve efficiency in group projects, universities should restrict the use of generative AI writing tools in collaborative assignments, because they weaken team fairness and academic integrity, undermine students’ authentic critical thinking and creativity, and prevent the development of essential teamwork skills.

AI writing tools damage fairness and academic honesty in group work. First, these tools make "free-riding" much easier than before. One lazy member can ask AI to write a full section and then paste the output. They appear just as busy as the rest of the team. When group members receive AI-generated drafts, they are still required to revise them for their teammates.This results in an overall workload that increases rather than decreases. Ultimately consuming more time. Next, AI-generated text appears fluent and grammatically correct, so teachers cannot easily tell who has truly understood the topic. Teammates are also unable to distinguish who has made genuine contributions and who has invested actual time and effort.  Group members using AI to complete group assignments without informing others puts the entire group at risk.The teacher discovers that a student's assignment was written by AI, every member of the group will face consequences and penalties ( UNESCO, 2023). A survey was conducted to understand more about the AI writing tools on group collaboration and a total of 22 completed questionnaires were returned. Our group survey collected 22 valid responses. The results back up all three points. Sixty percent of people are deeply concerned about being caught by teachers using artificial intelligence in their assignments.Many respondents pointed out  that a significant number of students are now directly copying and pasting AI-generated content into their coursework without any modifications.All in all, AI severely undermines team fairness and academic integrity.

Heavy use of AI writing tools also weakens students' own thinking and lowers the real value of their degrees. When a student asks ChatGPT to analyse a case, design an argument, or draft a plan, the student neglects the exact mental work and the process of remedying the setback that a group project was meant to build. At a fundamental level, it encourages individuals to use AI to remove practical obstacles and lower the threshold of finish assignment. Gerlich (2025) studied 666 participants and found that the high trend of using lead to the dearth of critical thinking due to the cognitive offloading. Kasneci et al. (2023) also warned that large language models can give smooth but shallow answers, which may hide the fact that the student never truly learned the topic. The ironic part is that Graduates often enter the workforce with high-level GPA but lower effective in real working state due to the deficient in innovation, judgment and adaptation, which leads to lower productivity and underperformance compared to expectations. This is particularly evident in Zhai’s study. The study conveys that for students who use AI frequently, their exhibiting increased laziness 68.9 % and 27.7% declined in decision-making abilities (Zhai, 2025). In our survey data about 95.6% interviewees use AI tools in above half of the group of the assignment. For all of the negative effect cause by using AI, as difficult to identify each member's actual contribution,  peer-evaluation becomes less reliable or reduces students' independent thinking skills... All interviewees address more than half of the respondents agreed on average. 31.8% among believe that "Loss of critical thinking and genuine discussion" is the most negative impact of using AI in group projects. In other words, students themselves are already aware of the long-term cost that the hidden skill gap will surface later in the future workplace. Consequently, this lays a solid foundation for fostering the doubting of high degree's reliability.

Unfortunately, AI writing tools take away the chance from students to build useful teamwork skills. Firstly, an excellent group project usually needs different ideas from groupmates to explore the feasibility of ideas. Group members can finish their project by providing ideas for each other and through the communication among members. If AI replaces thinking and communication among group members, the links that promote collaboration will disappear. This phenomenon can be shown in our survey. Among the options of "reducing group discussions", the average approval rating of the interviewed students reached about 3.23 (the approval level ranges from 1-5, the larger the number, the higher the approval level), which is the most recognized among the ten options for providing negative impact. Furthermore, real group work builds small but important habits. Members learn to split tasks, fix meeting times, merge documents, and resolve minor conflicts when plans change. These daily skills grow only through practice. When AI becomes the shortcut, those practice chances disappear. One respondent wrote in the open-comment box that "many students directly copy the data generated by AI into the document intact," which shows how fast the process can turn into simple copy-paste work. Kasneci et al. (2023) also noted that AI tools may replace, rather than support, the back-and-forth that makes collaborative learning work. As a result, the final file may look polished, yet the team behind it has learned little about being a real team.

Some people may think that AI writing tools can really help students, especially for the majority of Hong Kong students whose native language is not English. They point out that AI can speed up writing and automatically help students change grammar. AI writing tools seem to help writing in the short term. However, they cause more follow-up problems. Firstly, the main goal of a group assignment is not a polished final file. Group assignments are not just for submitting a collaborative assignment. Communication and joint thinking among group members during the process are also an important part. If students rely on AI-generated content to serve as group assignment content, communication among members will become dispensable and members will lose the opportunity to practice communication. A study conducted by Chan (2023) in Hong Kong found that both students and teachers wanted a clear policy framework for AI rather than free and open use. This means that most people do not want to lose the opportunity to improve communication and other abilities because of AI. Our survey also showed the same view. In our questionnaire, only 2 out of 22 respondents, or about 9% of all people, believed that AI can be used completely freely, this fully demonstrates that many people agree that AI tools will deprive students of many opportunities to hone their skills. Besides, reliance on AI tools will significantly reduce users’ independent critical thinking. Gerlich (2025) reports a significant decline in critical thinking skills among large numbers of AI users, the very skills employers want. Also, the  Journalistic accounts show this concern: Knapp (2025), writing in Forbes, summarized research showing that frequent use of AI assistants reduces students’ ability to think critically. Therefore, the simple statement that "AI helps students" is inconsistent with the evidence, and short-term help cannot compensate for long-term harm. All in all, the short-term convenience provided by AI writing tools does not excuse the long-term problems it brings and the negative impact of AI tools on students cannot be ignored.

Although AI writing tools appear to improve efficiency, university students in Hong Kong should try to minimize their reliance on them during group projects, this is because these tools undermine group fairness, weaken individual critical thinking skills, and hinder the development of genuine teamwork abilities. According to our research, approximately 40 to 50% of respondents believe that hidden plagiarism and false information are the most serious threats to group assignments. Furthermore, 91% of respondents expressed a desire for specific rules or additional oversight regarding the use of artificial intelligence. The harm to learning is real, and it spreads across fairness, thinking, and teamwork at the same time. First, every university in Hong Kong should publish a clear policy regarding the use of AI. This policy needs to distinguish where AI is permitted .For example, for spelling or grammar checks and where it is prohibited,such as using it to generate an entire thesis.Secondly, every group should be required to sign an AI usage declaration. For every group assignment, students must submit a process log documenting how they completed the work. An AI usage statement.These records will make it easier for professors to track whether students have used AI inappropriately. Teachers should add a presentation or defense component to every group assignment. This would allow them to test whether students truly understand the projects they are working on. Without these steps, future graduates may become skilled at prompting AI, yet stay weak at the real team thinking that employers value (Kasneci et al., 2023).
